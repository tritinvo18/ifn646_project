# Task 2: CRISPR Guide RNA Design and Multiplex Pool Selection

## Overview
Following the identification of differentially expressed genes in Task 1, this task focuses on designing highly specific and efficient CRISPR guide RNAs (gRNAs) to modulate their expression. 
* **CXCL11** (significantly upregulated) was selected for **CRISPR interference (CRISPRi)** to suppress its expression.
* **SERPINB2** (significantly downregulated) was selected for **CRISPR activation (CRISPRa)** to rescue its expression.

To maximize therapeutic efficacy and mitigate off-target risks, we computationally designed and filtered multiplexed pools of 4 gRNAs per target gene.

---

## Methods

### 1. Genomic Coordinate Mapping
To ensure that transcriptional modulation (CRISPRi/a) occurs exactly at the transcription start site, we targeted the first exon of each gene. The GRCh38 genomic GFF file was parsed to identify the exact coordinates of the first exon for the primary transcript of each gene (*NM_005409.5* for CXCL11; *NM_001143818.1* for SERPINB2). 

### 2. Sequence Extraction and Padding
Because a 20nt protospacer plus a 3nt PAM may extend outside the exon boundary while still directing the Cas9 cut site inside the exon, we extracted the first exon sequence alongside a ±25 base pair genomic flank using `samtools faidx`. This padded sequence was submitted to CRISPOR for exhaustive gRNA discovery.

### 3. Safety and Eligibility Filtering (Hard Filters)
Guides generated by CRISPOR were rigorously filtered. A guide was only marked as `eligible` if it passed all of the following absolute biological constraints:
* **Location:** The sequence mapped uniquely to the reference genome, and its predicted SpCas9 cut boundary was strictly contained *inside* the first exon.
* **Transcription Viability:** The guide did not contain a Poly-T tract (`TTTT`). In standard U6/U3 Pol III promoter systems, a Poly-T tract acts as a termination signal, which would result in a broken, prematurely truncated guide.
* **Toxicity / Off-Target Safety:** The guide achieved an industry-standard minimum specificity score of >50 for both MIT and CFD metrics, minimizing the risk of lethal off-target cleavage.
### 4. Ranking and Pool Selection (Soft Filters)
Eligible guides were penalized for structural inefficiencies (e.g., extreme GC content outside the 20-80% range, or TT-rich terminal motifs that induce biological instability). 
Guides were ranked using a custom **Composite Score**:
$$\text{Composite Score} = 0.60 \times \text{cfdSpecScore} + 0.40 \times \text{Efficiency}$$
The top 4 eligible guides for each gene were selected to form the final multiplex pools.

#### Rationale for the 60/40 Weighting (vs. 70/30, 80/20, or 50/50):
* **Asymmetric Risk Profile (Safety-First Principle):**
  In therapeutic transcriptome modulation (e.g., dampening CXCL11 hyperactivation or rescuing SERPINB2 in severe COVID-19), unintended off-target binding or cleavage can cause irreversible chromosomal aberrations or off-target silencing of tumor suppressors and essential genes. While modest on-target efficiency can be compensated by vector dosing and multiplexed guide synergy, off-target toxicity is biologically irreversible. Specificity must therefore hold the decisive majority (>50%) of the ranking power.
* **Pre-Conditioned Safety via Hard Filters:**
  High-risk off-target candidates are already pruned beforehand by our hard eligibility gates (`cfdSpecScore > 50` and `mitSpecScore > 50`). Because every guide entering the soft-ranking phase is already verified to have acceptable specificity, the ranking phase only needs to differentiate between "acceptable" (CFD ~79-83) and "exceptional" (CFD ~90+) specificity.
* **Preventing the "Inactive Guide" Pitfall (Why not 70/30 or 80/20):**
  Escalating specificity to 70% or 80% severely distorts candidate selection by heavily rewarding marginal specificity gains at the expense of biological cleavage kinetics. Many guide sequences achieve near-perfect specificity (>90) simply because they contain unusual k-mers that do not match the genome, yet these exact sequences often exhibit poor Cas9 binding and weak R-loop stability.
  * **Empirical proof from our CXCL11 pool:**
    * At **60/40**, the algorithm selects highly potent guides such as `261rev` (CFD: 79, Efficiency: **67**, Composite: 74.20) and `76forw` (CFD: 83, Efficiency: **65**, Composite: 75.80).
    * At **70/30** or **80/20**, `261rev` is discarded and replaced by `106forw` (CFD: 91, Efficiency: **42**). At 80/20, low-activity guides dominate the pool, creating a multiplex that is theoretically safe but clinically inactive.
* **Why Not 50/50?**
  A 50/50 equal weighting treats off-target liability and on-target cleavage potency as equivalent. In translational medicine and CRISPRi/a, safety must outweigh efficiency, necessitating a 1.5:1 ratio (60% to 40%).
* **The 60/40 Pareto Optimum:**
  A 60/40 distribution represents the optimal balance on the Pareto frontier: it maintains safety as the primary sorting driver while retaining a substantial 40% weight to ensure that selected guides have robust cleavage kinetics (Doench '16 > 50 wherever biologically feasible).

## Results

### CXCL11 (CRISPRi) Final Pool
The pipeline successfully identified a robust, highly specific pool of 4 guides targeting the first exon of CXCL11. None of the selected guides contained termination signals or structural warnings, and all achieved excellent composite scores.
* **76forw:** Cut boundary 249 (CFD: 83, Efficiency: 65)
* **264forw:** Cut boundary 61 (CFD: 89, Efficiency: 54)
* **261rev:** Cut boundary 55 (CFD: 79, Efficiency: 67)
* **295forw:** Cut boundary 30 (CFD: 91, Efficiency: 46)

### SERPINB2 (CRISPRa) Final Pool
The pipeline successfully identified 4 guides targeting the first exon of SERPINB2. While the guides passed all strict safety and location filters, the sequence architecture of the SERPINB2 locus resulted in slightly lower overall efficiency scores compared to CXCL11.
* **40forw:** Cut boundary 36 (CFD: 83, Efficiency: 68)
* **90forw:** Cut boundary 86 (CFD: 80, Efficiency: 71)
* **50rev:** Cut boundary 55 (CFD: 83, Efficiency: 51)
* **77rev:** Cut boundary 82 (CFD: 85, Efficiency: 37)

### Conclusion
We successfully designed two independent multiplex pools of 4 guides. Both pools strictly adhere to U6 transcription rules, guarantee exact first-exon targeting, and prioritize systemic off-target safety over raw cleavage efficiency. These guide pools were outputted to the `results/tables/` directory and advanced to Task 3 for population-scale genomic validation.
